01 Aureon Marketplace for authenticated luxury goods, with on-chain provenance 2026

A marketplace for watches, art and collectibles where buyers can trust what they are buying. Vetted sellers list items and get paid through Stripe Connect. Each listing can be minted as an ERC-721 “digital twin”, so its ownership history and authenticity check are recorded on Ethereum (Sepolia).

Buyers and sellers never touch a wallet: the server signs every on-chain write, and the app says so plainly when the chain is unreachable instead of faking success. Four roles, fourteen tables, row-level security on every one, and eighteen passing contract tests.

Supabase schema and row-level security, authentication, deployment. Team of four at APU.

02 LedgerLens An AI agent that reconciles cross-border payments, and shows its working 2026

A payment proof for USD 10,000 can show up on a Malaysian bank statement as RM 46,802 three days later, after conversion, bank margin and fees. LedgerLens matches the two anyway. It reasons about exchange rates, fee models, settlement drift and invoice references, and flags a row as uncertain instead of forcing a bad match.

The matching engine is deterministic and runs offline; only the rows it cannot settle are escalated to Claude through seven MCP tools. Money is handled as decimals throughout. Around 160 tests, including seven engineered trap cases.

Context
Built for an AI hackathon, for Malaysian SMEs.

03 NajmUni Study, money and paperwork in one place, for international students in Malaysia 2026

International students here juggle deadlines, a budget in a foreign currency, and visa papers that expire. NajmUni puts the three together and tells you what to do next. It runs in English and Arabic, right-to-left included.

It reads uploaded documents and bank statements, drafts the emails you would otherwise put off, and sends a weekly review. Every outside service is optional: with no keys at all it falls back to an embedded database and local sign-in, so the whole product runs on a laptop.
